## Findings

### A-01 — HIGH: a bound support read survives removal of the operator's support role
- **What:** `operator_scope_allows` (`drizzle/0046_support_session_rules.sql:122–139`) re-checks the vendor account, session, staff state and session scope on every row. It never re-checks `vendor_staff_roles`.
- **How it is reached:** `vendor_staff_update` deletes role rows (`0032:330–332`). An operator whose last support-capable role (`support`, `onboarding`, `platform_owner`) was removed keeps reading inside an already-bound transaction until the session ends.
- **Rule:** IAM-16 (`docs/spec/07-IAM-RBAC-ABAC.md:30`). Removing a role invalidates affected access within 60 s.
- **Why the rest is safe:** new binds check the role (`support_read_caller`, `0046:218–225`).

### A-02 / A-02x / N-2 — HIGH: an actor's validity is judged at transaction start or before the staff lock
- **The pattern:** many SECURITY DEFINER functions check "an active membership holding the acting role" with `m.effective_from <= now() AND (m.effective_to IS NULL OR m.effective_to > now())`. Some do it before taking the tenant's `staff_access` lock, or take no lock at all.
- **Why that is wrong:** `now()` is the transaction start. `staff_end_membership` ends a membership at `clock_timestamp()` under `staff_access` (`0026:197`). A transaction that started before the end, or waited on the lock while the end committed, still sees the membership as active and keeps acting.
- **The fix already in the codebase:** `staff_admin()` (`0026:37–39`) takes the lock first and then uses `clock_timestamp()`.
- **Where (latest definitions):**
  - `role_assign` (`0036:302`): actor and target checks with `now()`; the target check runs after the lock.
  - `role_request_decide` (`0036:361`): actor check with `now()` before the lock; subject check with `now()` after it.
  - `role_owner_confirm` (`0036:416`) and `role_owner_grant_withdraw` (`0036:478`): `approval_actor()` before the lock.
  - `approval_actor()` (`0022:34`, `now()`, no lock), with these callers:
    - `approval_request`, `approval_decide` (`0036:68`), `approval_owner_self`, `approval_cancel`;
    - `setting_set` (`0028:342`), `setting_draft_decide`;
    - `evidence_upload` (`0030:284`), `evidence_decide` (`0036:156`, lock taken later at `:186`);
    - `vendor_dual_identity_clinic_decide` (`0032:650`, no `staff_access` lock);
    - the read-only `compliance_register`, `vendor_dual_identities` and `support_owner_reader`.
  - `audit_append` (`0011:62–67`, `now()`).
  - `notification_ack` (`0024:150`, `now()`).
  - The `org_actions_insert` policy (`0009:21`, `now()`).
  - **Vendor session read before the lock:** `support_caller` (`0046:170–188`) and `onboarding_grant_check` (`0044:~128`) read `vendor_session(token)` before `staff_access`. `vendor_session` uses `expires_at > now()`. `vendor_guard`'s step-up age is judged against `now()` (`0032:135`).
- **Rules:** IAM-16; the codebase's own invariant in `0026:37–39`.

### R1-H2 — HIGH (plan review round 1, GPT): `audit_append` authorizes before its serializing lock
- `audit_append` (`0011:29–75`) checks the actor's membership and role, then takes the per-tenant audit lock (`0011:~74`).
- `evidence_file_content` (`0030:411–424`) calls it without holding `staff_access`.
- So a download whose caller waited on the audit lock while their membership was ended and committed still returns the file.
- Using `clock_timestamp()` alone does not fix this: the check must be **repeated after** the audit lock.

### R1-M2 — MEDIUM (plan review round 1, GPT): other support and vendor checks are judged on the transaction-start clock or are missing
- `support_read_caller` (`0046:217`): the second-factor age is checked against `now()`.
- `vendor_console_guard` (`0034:38–49`) duplicates `vendor_guard`'s checks with `now()`; it has to match `vendor_guard` (refusal de-duplication, tests/vendor/host.test.ts).
- `operator_scope_allows` does not re-check TOTP enrolment, unlike a new bind (`totp_required`).
- `vendor_guard` takes `FOR SHARE` on `vendor_staff` (`0032:125–135`). That lock can wait, and a sign-out during the wait is not re-checked.

### R1-M3 — MEDIUM (plan review round 1, claude): `staff_invite_accept` checks its inviter with no serializing lock
- It locks the invitation row and calls `staff_inviter_may_grant` without `staff_access` (`0026:438–440`).
- An inviter whose membership is being ended in an uncommitted transaction still grants the membership. The end's `staff_invitations_recheck` then finds the invitation already accepted.

### R1-L1 — LOW (plan review round 1, GPT): owner-invitation expiry is checked before a `staff_access` wait
- `tenant_owner_invite_accept` (`0040:300–306`) locks the invitation row and checks `expires_at` against `now()`, then waits on `staff_access`, and never re-checks.

### A-03 — MEDIUM: the expiry sweep can end a session whose extension committed while it waited
- `support_session_expire` (`0046:539–556`) selects expired candidates **before** taking each tenant's `staff_access` lock.
- `support_session_end` (`0046:322–336`) ends a session without re-checking expiry.
- So an extension (`vendor_console_support_extend`, `0046:310–312`, under `staff_access`) that commits while the sweep waits on the lock is overridden.

### B-01 — MEDIUM: the operator role reads columns the app role is denied (TEN-19 projections)
- `clinic_app` has column grants that exclude:
  - `evidence_files.content` (`0030:93–95`);
  - `staff_invitations.token_hash` (`0026:20–22`).
- `clinic_operator` gets `GRANT SELECT` on the whole tables (`0046:585`, `0046:618`; `src/db/rls/operator.sql`).
- Rule TEN-19: operator predicates check approved object kinds **and projections**.

### B-02 — LOW: the "ending a membership is always allowed" branch never fires
- `memberships_vendor_guard` (`0032:222`) skips its dual-identity check only when `NEW.effective_to <= now()`.
- An end writes `clock_timestamp()`, which is always later than `now()`, so the branch is unreachable.
- Offboarding a vendor-staff member with a clinic membership would be refused once dual identities can be revoked (T-028).

### B-03 — LOW: a global identity sequence is readable by tenants
- `evidence_approvals.seq` (`0029:16`) is a table-wide identity, readable by `clinic_app` (`0030:93`).
- Its gaps reveal how many evidence decisions other tenants made.

### B-04 — LOW: `membership_revocations` has no TRUNCATE guard
- `0026:522` covers UPDATE and DELETE only.
- Every other history table has a `*_no_truncate` trigger.

## Change: one migration, `drizzle/0048_authority_wall_clock.sql` (expand only: one nullable column, functions, grants, triggers, one policy; no data change)

1. **A wall-clock actor check after the lock.**
   - New `public.tenant_actor_lock(OUT tenant_id, OUT staff_id, OUT role, OUT branch_id)`:
     - is declared **`VOLATILE`** (plan review round 3, claude M-1). A `STABLE` function reads with the calling statement's snapshot, taken before the lock wait, so it would miss an end committed during the wait.
     - takes `staff_access` for the bound tenant;
     - then, as a **separate statement**, verifies the actor with the same membership, role and branch rule as `approval_actor`, using `clock_timestamp()`.
     - raises `42501` with the message `ERR_FORBIDDEN: tenant_actor_lock: bound context is not an active membership with that role and branch`.
     - **Grants:** `REVOKE ALL FROM PUBLIC`, and no grant to `clinic_app`. Only the definer writers call it, so no app caller can hold a tenant's `staff_access` through it.
   - `role_assign` and `role_request_decide` also call `tenant_actor_lock` at entry, in place of their own checks. Their `owner`/`admin_pusat` role rule stays after it. So every writer refuses with the one identifiable message.
   - `approval_actor()` keeps its signature. It now uses `clock_timestamp()` for both bounds. It takes no lock, because read functions call it (`compliance_register`, `vendor_dual_identities`, `support_owner_reader`, `role_owner_grants_mine`, `evidence_file_content`). No policy calls it.
   - The writers that today call `approval_actor()` before any lock call `tenant_actor_lock()` instead. Each is re-created with `CREATE OR REPLACE`, with the body otherwise unchanged:
     - `approval_request`, `approval_decide`, `approval_owner_self`, `approval_cancel`;
     - `setting_set`;
     - `evidence_upload`, `evidence_decide`;
     - `role_owner_confirm`, `role_owner_grant_withdraw`;
     - `vendor_dual_identity_clinic_decide`.
     - Where a body takes `staff_access` again later, the second call is harmless (reentrant xact lock) and stays.
   - **Lock order:** `staff_access` before every row lock, which is the order `role_assign`, `staff_admin_lock` and the support functions already use.
     - **Known inversion that must be fixed in the same migration:** `setting_draft_decide` (`0028:448`) locks the approval row **before** it calls `approval_decide`. If `approval_decide` then took `staff_access`, that path would be approval row → `staff_access`, while every other path is `staff_access` → approval row: a deadlock.
     - So `setting_draft_decide` **and `setting_draft_cancel`** (`0028:460–471`: approval row, then `approval_cancel`; plan review round 1, GPT HIGH) take `staff_access` first, then the approval row. Every function the migration re-creates is listed with its lock sequence in the migration comment, and each sequence begins with `staff_access`. The existing order after it stays: approval row → draft row → setting lock → audit.
     - Every caller of each re-created function is listed too (`grep` of `drizzle/` and `src/` for each name), so no existing path takes a row lock before calling one of them. The review found these callers:
       - `setting_set` → `approval_request`, which re-enters harmlessly;
       - `setting_draft_decide`, `setting_draft_cancel` (both fixed);
       - `src/modules/approvals/approvals.ts:36–58` and `src/modules/settings/settings.ts:164–167`, which take no prior row lock.
     - **Already-inverted paths this migration fixes or names:**
       - `evidence_decide` takes the item `FOR UPDATE` (`0036:157`), then `staff_access` (`:188`). It is fixed by `tenant_actor_lock` at entry.
       - `tenant_owner_invite_accept` takes the invitation row, then `staff_access` (`0040:300–306`). It is kept: no path takes `staff_access` and then that row, since the Owner trigger only runs when no invitation is issued. The comment names it as an exception.
       - **Outside this task, named:** `vendor_console_owner_invite_approve` locks `tenants_registry FOR UPDATE` then the invitation row (`0040:172–173`). `tenant_owner_invite_accept` locks the invitation row and later takes a key share on `tenants_registry` through `vendor_log`'s foreign key (`0040:336`).
         - An approve on an already-issued invitation running beside an accept can deadlock.
         - It is recorded in BUILD-LOG and filed for T-032 (M0 exit review) to fix or accept, because it is a vendor-console path outside this task's findings.
     - **Rule for later code**, recorded in DECISIONS and ARCHITECTURE:
       - No caller holds a row lock before calling a writer that takes `staff_access`.
       - `feature_gate_lock` (`0030:195–204`, `FOR SHARE` on evidence items) is called only after `staff_access`, or in a transaction that calls no such writer.
       - `vendor_guard` never takes `staff_access`.
       - **Audit acquisition order:** a path first acquires `staff_access` (when it takes it) before it first acquires the tenant's audit lock. Re-entering a lock already held does not count. A path never waits on `staff_access` while holding that tenant's audit lock. Audit can come before a later write in the same path (onboarding checks, `role_owner_grants_lapse`): that is allowed.
       - **Exception:** the expiry sweep holds tenant A's audit lock while it takes tenant B's `staff_access`. It visits tenants in id order, so no cycle can form.
     - A test proves no deadlock: two concurrent decides on different approvals of one tenant, a setting draft decide and a staff change all finish.
2. **`role_assign` and `role_request_decide`** take `staff_access` first. Then both the actor and the target or subject are checked with `clock_timestamp()`.
3. **`audit_append`:**
   - It uses `clock_timestamp()`.
   - It **re-checks the actor after taking the audit lock**: the same membership, role and branch query, run again right after `pg_advisory_xact_lock('audit:' …)`. It raises `42501` if the actor is no longer valid.
   - It does not take `staff_access`, so the order stays `staff_access` → audit everywhere.
   - `evidence_file_content` therefore refuses a download whose caller was ended while it waited on the audit lock, because the audit append runs before the `RETURN QUERY`.
   - `notification_ack` and the `org_actions_insert` policy (`drizzle/0009_org_actions_rls.sql:21`) use `clock_timestamp()`; they are low-impact and take no lock.
4. **Vendor session after every blocking lock:**
   - `vendor_session`, `vendor_guard`'s second-factor age check, `support_read_caller` (`0046:217`) and `vendor_console_guard`'s copied checks (`0034:38–49`) use `clock_timestamp()`. They stay in lockstep with `vendor_guard`, so the refusal de-duplication still matches.
   - `support_caller`, `onboarding_grant_check`, `vendor_console_guard`, `vendor_console_writer`, **`vendor_console_staff_list`** (`0042:33`) and **`vendor_console_staff_state`** (`0042:132`) re-read `vendor_session(token)` **after** their last blocking wait. They refuse with `no_session` if it ended meanwhile.
     - The last blocking wait is `vendor_guard`'s `FOR SHARE` on `vendor_staff` (after any `staff_access` wait).
     - For `vendor_console_staff_state`, see `vendor_staff_update_checked` below.
   - **`vendor_guard`** reads `auth_user.two_factor_enabled` **after** its `FOR SHARE` on `vendor_staff`, together with the `clock_timestamp()` age check. It no longer reads it before.
   - **What is re-checked (plan review round 5, GPT):** not only that the session exists. A new helper `vendor_authority_recheck(p_token, p_roles, p_max_age)` (`VOLATILE`, `SECURITY DEFINER`, no grant to `clinic_vendor`, executable only by the definer functions) re-reads at the final authorization point:
     - the session through `vendor_session`;
     - its `second_factor_at` against the same `p_max_age` the path's `vendor_guard` used (10 min for writes, 12 h for reads), on `clock_timestamp()`;
     - `auth_user.two_factor_enabled`;
     - the staff row's `active` state;
     - a held role in `p_roles`.
     - **How it refuses** (plan review round 6, claude M-1):
       - It raises SQLSTATE `42501` with the message `ERR_FORBIDDEN: vendor_authority: <reason>`, where `<reason>` is one of `vendor_guard`'s codes (`no_session`, `totp_not_verified`, `totp_required`, `vendor_staff_inactive`, `role_not_held`). The raise rolls back the transaction's effects.
       - It takes no lock.
       - **The refusal is still logged** (SEC-16; `0032:110–111`). The four TS `call` helpers catch exactly this error and then:
         - commit the refusal in a **separate transaction** through a new `vendor_authority_refused(p_token, p_ip, p_action, p_tenant, p_reason)` (definer, executable by `clinic_vendor`, reasons limited to the list above). This is the pattern of `vendor_request_consume_refused` (`0032:630`). It writes the `vendor_actions` row and, for a tenant, the tenant chain;
         - return `{ ok: false, code: "ERR_FORBIDDEN", reason }`. With `no_session`, the console signs the user out, as it does today.
         - The four helpers: `src/modules/vendor/tenants/tenants.ts`, `team/team.ts`, `support/support.ts`, `onboarding/onboarding.ts`. They are in the Allowed Paths.
       - **Never inside an `EXCEPTION` block:** `vendor_console_tenant_advance` catches `42501` around its transition (`0044:388–391`), and `support_ticket` catches `unique_violation`. The re-check is placed outside every handler, so it is never turned into a domain refusal. The structural test checks it.
     - Every "re-read" below means a call to this helper, not to `vendor_session` alone.
   - **The rule (simplified in plan review round 6, after claude round 5 M-1; made exact in round 7, GPT round 6):**
     - A vendor entry point calls `vendor_authority_recheck` **after its last statement that can wait or have an effect, and before every exit that can keep an effect or return data**. That includes refusal exits taken after an effect: `vendor_console_staff_invite` and `vendor_console_owner_invite` expire invitations, then may return a domain refusal.
     - Exits taken by the initial guard (refusals before any effect) are unchanged.
     - **Readers build their response first:** the complete response is materialized into a local variable, then the re-check runs as a separate statement, then the function returns the variable. A re-check placed before a `RETURN (SELECT …)` would still be before the read.
     - Every reader that returns tenant or staff data is in scope: `vendor_console_me`, `_tenants`, `_tenant_detail`, `_support_list`, `_staff_list`, `_onboarding_access`.
     - `vendor_console_tenant_create` too: it writes only new rows, but its final re-check costs nothing and keeps the rule without exceptions.
     - A refusal there raises, so the whole transaction rolls back, and an effect already written is undone.
     - Every wait inside the function comes before this point: explicit locks, `FOR UPDATE`/`FOR SHARE`, implicit row waits of an `UPDATE`/`DELETE`, foreign-key `FOR KEY SHARE` waits of an `INSERT`, and unique-index waits. So the rule cannot miss a wait, whatever its kind.
     - The only statements after it are the success `vendor_log` and the return. Their wait only delays the log row of an effect that was authorized after every earlier wait; that was accepted in round 3.
     - Refusals that the guard already logged and returned are unchanged.
     - The per-path placements below were the earlier, narrower rule. They are kept as the list of waits each test must exercise, but the placement for every path is now "last, before return".
   - The bullets that follow list, per path, **the waits each test must exercise**. They are not placements: every placement is given by the rule above. For `vendor_console_tenant_create` and the guarded reads, the table's "not needed" means "no wait"; they still get the re-check, as the rule says. Where a path waits again after the re-read, it re-reads again just before its write:
     - `vendor_console_onboarding_invite` re-reads after `onboarding_grant_check` returns. That check ends with `vendor_log`, which can wait on the tenant audit lock. The re-read goes **after** its existing `onboarding_grant_active` re-check, which takes the grant row `FOR SHARE` and can itself wait, and just before the draft insert.
     - `vendor_console_owner_invite_approve` (`0040:171–173`) re-reads after its `tenants_registry FOR UPDATE` and invitation-row locks, before it approves.
